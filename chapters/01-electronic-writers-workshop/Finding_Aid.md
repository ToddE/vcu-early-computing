# Finding Aid: The SHARE / Electronic Writers' Workshop Collection

## Repository
Held privately by A. Todd Emerson (VCU account `has0ate`); prepared for potential
accession by VCU Libraries and/or the College of Humanities and Sciences.

## Collection Title
The SHARE / Electronic Writers' Workshop (EWW) Development Collection

## Dates
1990–1993 (bulk 1991–1993)

## Extent
61 text files (~230 KB), recovered from two backup copies of a VCU DEC UNIX
home directory (account `has0ate`, English Department, VCU). Files are plain
ASCII, c-shell script source, troff/nroff-formatted documents, and RFC-822
email messages preserved in mbox-style plaintext.

## Creator / Provenance
A. Todd Emerson, VCU undergraduate (English Department), account holder
1990–1993. Files were created and maintained on VCU's Cabell/Hibbs DEC UNIX
systems (`cabell.vcu.edu`, `hibbs.vcu.edu`) as part of an Undergraduate
Research Grant project and a subsequent SCHEV (State Council of Higher
Education for Virginia) grant, both supervised by Dr. Ann M. Woodlief,
English Department, VCU. Recovered by the creator from two independent
backup copies of the account made at the time; both copies are retained
as sub-collections and cross-checked against each other for completeness.

## Biographical / Historical Note
Beginning around 1990, Todd Emerson, then a VCU undergraduate, received an
Undergraduate Research Grant to develop "Computers and Prewriting" (later
"Computers and Freewriting"), a computer-assisted prewriting heuristic for
students in English 101/102. The project was supervised by Dr. Ann M.
Woodlief and supported technically by Michael A. Keller, Computer
Coordinator of the VCU Writing/Computer Center, and by Gregory S. DeBruyn
("Greg," VCU's SC Mgr / systems consultant), who contributed c-shell and
terminal-control programming assistance.

Out of this work grew SHARE, a menu-driven c-shell program allowing
students on VCU's networked DEC systems to submit ("share"), retrieve
("grab"), and turn in papers electronically to a shared class folder
structure, integrated with WordPerfect 5.0 via keyboard macros. SHARE was
later formalized, packaged, and copyrighted as the **Electronic Writers'
Workshop (EWW)**, with a full user manual, installation instructions, and
folder/version-numbering system, and was used by VCU English Department
faculty (notably Dr. Woodlief, who used it to post discussion questions
and collect reading responses) into the mid-1990s, prior to general
web-browser adoption. Emerson continued the work under a SCHEV grant
through 1993, which funded expanded disk quota and continued development.

This predates the World Wide Web's general availability and represents an
early, locally built example of networked, collaborative courseware —
essentially a text-based learning management system — built by an
undergraduate in partnership with English faculty and campus computing
staff.

Of particular note: correspondence in Series 4 (`AWOOD`, Feb 1993) shows
Dr. Woodlief directly lobbying Steven A. Saltzberg of Academic Campus
Computing Services (ACCS) to formally recognize and support Emerson's
program, and documents a subsequent presentation of SHARE to the VCU
Faculty User Group (FUG) — a meeting of faculty, administrators, and
computing staff, including Saltzberg — on Feb 25, 1993. Saltzberg later
went on to co-create **Web Course in a Box**, an early web-based course
management system developed at VCU, and MadDuck Technologies. Whether
this FUG presentation and the surrounding exchange influenced that later
work is not established here, but the documentary link — Saltzberg
personally administering Emerson's account, being lobbied on the
program's significance, and attending its formal demonstration — is
worth flagging for anyone researching Web Course in a Box's origins.
Michael Keller's surviving email archive (not yet examined) may contain
further relevant correspondence from this period.

## Scope and Content
The collection documents the design, implementation, and classroom
deployment of SHARE/EWW, and the grant-funded prewriting-software research
that gave rise to it. It is organized into four series:

- **Series 1: SHARE System Source** — the original c-shell menu-driven
  program and its iterations/backups (grab, share, submit, exit
  functions), plus supporting utility scripts (trash management, file
  listing/display, prompt customization). Includes a message from VCU
  Academic Campus Computing Services (Steven Saltzberg) confirming
  quota-extension correspondence.
- **Series 2: EWW System Documentation** — the packaged, copyrighted
  release: copyright/registration files, folder- and version-numbering
  scripts, setup script, and the end-user manual describing the
  WordPerfect-macro integration (`PF3 KP2` → `share`). Includes
  `STAFF.INF`, a plain-language description of "What is Grab & Share?"
  written for instructors, describing Dr. Woodlief's actual classroom use.
- **Series 3: Grant and Research Documentation** — the SCHEV disk-quota
  request letter, the Undergraduate Research Grant proposal and progress
  reports, grant program documentation, and `GRANT3.PAS`, a Pascal source
  file representing an early prototype of the prewriting-heuristic
  program itself.
- **Series 4: Development Correspondence** — email correspondence
  documenting the collaborative build process: the full mailbox of
  correspondence with Dr. Ann Woodlief (139 messages, Oct 1992–Jun 1993);
  correspondence with Michael Keller; correspondence with Gregory DeBruyn
  (including an ANSI terminal escape-code tutorial used in the SHARE
  interface, and a shared "trashcan" utility script); a note from the
  "Hibbs Site Manager" account asking Emerson for technical help; a
  consulting-work summary describing the TUTOR vi-editor package built
  with Keller for the English Department; and English Department
  listserv traffic from the era.

## Arrangement
Four series as described above, each in its own subdirectory. Files
retain their original VCU DEC filenames (8.3-style truncated names typical
of the system). Where two backup copies contained the same file, the more
complete version was selected.

## Related Material Not Included
The source account also contained substantial undergraduate coursework
(a folklore fieldwork project on Deerfield, Virginia; annotated notes on
T.S. Eliot's *The Waste Land*; film-course essays; poetry) and personal
correspondence and files unrelated to educational technology. These were
excluded from this collection as being of primarily personal rather than
institutional/archival interest, but are retained by the creator and can
be made available if the folklore project is of interest as a separate
digital-humanities accession.

## Access and Use

headers and bodies; the creator should be consulted before any public

## A note on redaction

All email addresses, phone numbers, and the creator's home address have
been redacted from every file in this collection (replaced with
`[email redacted]`, `[phone redacted]`, `[address redacted]`).
Correspondents are identified by name in prose, in the finding aid, and
in message headers instead. Passages and messages concerning purely
personal matters unrelated to the SHARE/EWW project — social plans,
hobbies, other mailing lists and interest groups the correspondents ran
or belonged to — have also been removed. `MKELLER` in particular has
been reduced from its original ~2,000-line mailbox to a short curated
excerpt of project-relevant passages only, with an editorial note at the
top of the file explaining the cut. A separate personal-poetry mailing
list file (`ENG.POE`) that had no bearing on SHARE/EWW was removed from
the collection entirely rather than redacted.
